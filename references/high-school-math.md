# High-School Mathematics Profile

Use for definitions, theorems, formulas, functions, geometric relationships,
probability, calculus, vectors, and question types within the high-school
curriculum.

## Required Content

- exact definition, symbols, domain, and conditions
- prerequisite concept if it prevents a likely misunderstanding
- central property, theorem, or formula, with conditions clearly attached
- intuitive explanation of the relationship
- one worked example with enough steps to show the method
- a diagram, graph, table, or transformation that reveals the structure
- a common boundary case, misconception, or invalid condition when useful
- one self-check question or transfer cue

## Teaching Notes

Do not present formulas without their applicable conditions. Keep proof,
intuition, and computation visibly separate. Verify notation and examples; a
card can simplify, but it cannot silently change a theorem.

## Visual Pattern

Prefer coordinate axes, geometric construction, number line, function
transformation, area model, tree diagram, or variable relationship map. Make
orange encode the variable, interval, angle, or relation being discussed.

## Card Layout

- top: concept and learning objective
- left: definition and conditions
- center: diagram or graph with exact labels
- right or bottom: worked example and error check
